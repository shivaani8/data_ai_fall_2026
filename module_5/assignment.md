# Data Storytelling Assignment: Dashboard Makeover

_Due: Saturday October 10th· Estimated time to complete: 3 hours_

## Overview

You've already built a dashboard. It holds a lot of useful information, but a dashboard is made for **exploring**: the viewer has to work out what matters on their own.

A **data story** is made for **explaining**. It walks your audience through the data one step at a time, points out what's important, and ends with a clear takeaway.

In this assignment, you'll take a dashboard you've already made and **"makeover"** it into a data story.

| Dashboard | Data Story |
| --- | --- |
| Shows many charts at once | Shows one idea at a time |
| Viewer decides what's important | You guide the viewer to the insight |
| Mostly charts and filters | Charts plus titles, text, and context |
| Answers "What's happening?" | Answers "What's happening, why does it matter, and what should we do?" |

## Step 2: Find Your Story

Before you build anything, look closely at your dashboard and ask:

- **Who is my audience?** (A manager? A nonprofit? The general public?)
- **What is the single most important thing I want them to know?**
- **What surprised me in this data?**
- **What should my audience do or think differently after seeing this?**

Write a **one-sentence big idea** that sums up your story.

> **Example:** _"Moms are leaving the workforce at higher rates than dads, and the main reason is childcare costs, so employers should consider offering childcare support."_

Then pick **3–5 key insights** from your dashboard that back up your big idea. Each insight becomes one slide or one page.

- [ ] I've written my one-sentence big idea
- [ ] I've chosen 3–5 insights that support it

## Step 3: Choose Your Format

You can build your data story in **one** of two ways:

### Option A: Slide Deck Presentation

Put your charts into a slide deck (Google Slides, PowerPoint, Canva, etc.) to present like a talk.

- Take screenshots of your Looker charts, or rebuild them, and put **one chart per slide**
- Give each slide a title that states the insight, not just the topic
- Add short text, arrows, or highlights to point out what matters

### Option B: Directly in Looker

Build your story inside Looker Studio by adding new pages to your report.

- Add a **new page** for each insight (Page → New page)
- Put **one main visualization** on each page, along with a text box explaining it
- Use the page names and navigation so viewers can move through the story in order

## Step 4: Build Your Story

Use this structure as a guide:

| Section | What to Include | Example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **1. Title / Hook** | A title and an opening that grabs attention | "1 in 3 moms left the workforce in 2021. Why?" |
| **2. Context** | Background on the topic and your data source | Where the data came from, the time period, and why it matters |
| **3. Insights (3–5)** | One chart per slide/page, with a title that states the takeaway | "Childcare costs rose 41% in 10 years" |
| **4. Conclusion / Call to Action** | Your big idea, plus what the audience should do next | "Employers offering childcare support keep 2x more moms" |

### Makeover Tips

Use what you learned from **Remove to Improve** and **Storytelling with Data Makeovers**:

- ✂️ **Remove clutter:** cut gridlines, extra labels, borders, and 3D effects
- 🎯 **Highlight what matters:** make the key data point stand out with color, and gray out the rest
- 📝 **Use headlines that state the insight:** "Sales dropped 20% in Q3" instead of "Sales by Quarter"
- 📊 **Choose the right chart:** a line for trends over time, a bar for comparisons, and skip the pie chart if there are more than 3 slices
- 👁️ **Show one idea at a time:** if a slide makes two points, split it into two slides
- 🤝 **Be ethical and inclusive:** cite your data source, avoid misleading axes, and use colorblind-friendly colors
